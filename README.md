## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution

   We propose KisanConnect, an AI-powered digital agricultural marketplace that directly connects farmers, Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs), retailers, wholesalers, restaurants, and consumers.

The platform eliminates unnecessary intermediaries by allowing farmers to list their agricultural products directly on the marketplace. Buyers can browse products, compare prices, place orders, and make secure digital payments.

The platform also provides:

Direct farmer-to-consumer buying and selling.

Bulk purchasing options for wholesalers and retailers.

Real-time agricultural product pricing.

AI-based demand forecasting.

Smart logistics and delivery management.

Farmer and buyer verification.

Order tracking and digital payment facilities.

Reduces the number of intermediaries in the agricultural supply chain.

Allows farmers to receive a higher share of the selling price.

Helps consumers purchase fresh products at affordable prices.

Connects farmers with bulk buyers.

Reduces post-harvest wastage through better demand prediction.

Improves transportation and delivery efficiency.

## Feasibility and Viability
 Feasibility and Viability
Analysis of feasibility
The proposed platform is technically and economically feasible because it uses existing technologies such as web development frameworks, cloud databases, AI libraries, digital payments, and mapping services.

Technical feasibility: Can be developed using open-source technologies.

Economic feasibility: Initial development can be done with minimal infrastructure costs.

Operational feasibility: Farmers and buyers can access the system using smartphones.

Scalability: Can expand from local markets to district, state, and national levels.

Potential challenges and risks
Lack of digital literacy among some farmers.

Internet connectivity issues in rural areas.

Difficulty maintaining product quality during transportation.

Price fluctuations in agricultural markets.

Trust and payment-related issues.

Availability of reliable logistics partners.

Strategies to overcome challenges
Provide a simple multilingual interface.

Include voice-assisted navigation.

Support low-bandwidth and mobile-friendly access.

Introduce verified farmer and buyer profiles.

Provide transparent pricing and transaction records.

Partner with local delivery providers and FPOs.
